Financial reporting quality conversations tend to center on auditors, systems, or regulation. New research points to a factor that gets far less attention: the IT skills of the employees actually producing the reports.

"As financial reporting becomes increasingly digital, we believe our findings can inform hiring, training and talent strategy decisions, as well as encourage policymakers and educators to support the development of IT skills in the financial workforce," said Adi Masli, Larry D. Horner/KPMG Professor at the University of Kansas School of Business.

His paper, "Access to IT-capable employees and the relevance of financial information," appears in the Journal of Accounting and Public Policy. Co-written with J. Philipp Klaus of the University of Texas at Arlington and Pradeep Sapkota of the University of North Texas, it examines how IT-capable employees shape the production of financial information.

## Two ways IT skill improves reporting

Masli's team found access to IT-capable employees helps in two distinct ways: it improves the accuracy of XBRL tagging (the standardized format used in financial disclosures), and it supports the broader information systems that keep a company's underlying data consistent and accurate.

> "Ultimately, this leads to more timely and reliable financial disclosures."

Since companies don't publicly report their workforce's IT competency, the team measured it indirectly — using labor-market data for the IT workforce surrounding each company's headquarters as a proxy for the quality of IT talent available to that firm.

## Relevance, not just reliability

The study builds on earlier work by Masli showing that IT-capable employees reduce financial misstatements — a reliability question. This paper asks a different question: does that same access also make disclosures more relevant and timely, not just more accurate? The answer, across the sample, was yes — with no apparent tradeoff between the two goals. As Masli put it, strong IT talent "can enhance both dimensions of reporting quality," a result he frames as a genuine competitive edge, not just a compliance benefit.
