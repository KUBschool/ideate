The GameStop short squeeze of early 2021 became a cultural moment almost as much as a financial one — a story about retail investors, a struggling video game retailer, and hedge funds caught badly offside. New research puts numbers behind the narrative, tracing exactly how online discussion translated into market-moving trading behavior.

Using large-scale text data from Reddit, StockTwits and X, the study measures how surges in retail-investor conversation lined up with trading volume and volatility in GameStop, AMC and a handful of other heavily shorted stocks. The relationship wasn't just correlation after the fact — spikes in coordinated discussion reliably preceded the sharpest price moves, suggesting the platforms weren't merely reacting to the squeeze but actively organizing it.

## Why this matters beyond one meme stock

The research reframes "meme stock" events less as a one-off curiosity and more as a preview of how retail coordination can function at scale when the right stock, the right narrative, and the right platform line up. For regulators and exchanges, that has implications for how market oversight accounts for social-media-driven trading — a channel that barely existed the last time short-squeeze dynamics were studied closely.
