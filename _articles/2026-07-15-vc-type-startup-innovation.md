Founders often treat venture capital as a fairly uniform resource: money, plus some advice and connections. New research suggests the source of that money matters more than founders may realize — specifically, whether it comes from an independent VC firm or a corporate venture arm attached to an established company.

Comparing startups across both funding types, the study finds systematically different patterns in how portfolio companies pursue co-innovation — partnering with other firms to jointly develop new products or technology. Corporate VC backing tends to pull startups toward partnerships that align with the parent company's strategic interests, while independent VC backing leaves startups freer to pursue partnerships purely on commercial merit.

## Neither type is simply "better"

The research is careful not to crown one funding source superior — the different innovation outcomes reflect different incentive structures, not different skill levels. For founders, the practical takeaway is to think about VC type as a strategic input to the business, not just a financing decision, since it can shape which partnerships even come into view years down the road.
