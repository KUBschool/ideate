The accounting profession's talent pipeline problem is well-documented and not easy to fix with any single lever. New research tests one specific, low-cost intervention directly — and finds it works, but not for everyone the same way.

Amanda Winn, associate professor of accounting at the University of Kansas, co-authored "How Sustainability Accounting Emphasis Can Help Improve Perceptions of Accounting Careers and Grow the Accounting Pipeline" with E. Horne, S. Loftus, and S.S. McCoy, published in Current Issues in Auditing.

## The same career, framed two different ways

The research design is straightforward: show Generation Z business students two versions of an accounting career description — one emphasizing sustainability assurance work, one emphasizing traditional financial assurance — and measure the difference in reported interest. Students consistently reported greater interest in accounting careers after seeing the sustainability-focused version.

## The effect isn't universal — it's values-driven

The more interesting part of the finding is who's actually responding to the framing shift. The increased interest wasn't spread evenly across all students; it was concentrated among students with stronger prosocial preferences — those who place real weight on contributing to others' wellbeing through their work. Students who were more self-focused showed no comparable jump in interest between the two versions.

> "These students are more attracted to sustainability-focused accounting roles because they see a clearer alignment between the work and their personal values."

## Why "one version fits everyone" was never the right frame

That split matters for how the finding should actually be used. It's not evidence that sustainability framing makes accounting more broadly appealing to Gen Z as a whole — it's evidence that accounting's existing appeal gap with a specific, values-motivated segment of students can be closed by describing the work in terms that are already true, just not usually emphasized. The researchers frame this as a genuine values-alignment effect, not a marketing trick: sustainability assurance work really does connect to prosocial motivation, and simply saying so out loud changes how students weigh it.

## A low-cost lever for firms and educators

For firms and accounting programs trying to grow the talent pipeline, the practical takeaway is narrow but useful: recruiting materials that genuinely highlight the sustainability-related dimensions of accounting work are likely to resonate with a meaningful, identifiable segment of students — without needing to claim something untrue about the profession to do it. The research is also a broader case against one-size-fits-all recruiting messaging, since the same description that moves one group barely moves another at all.
