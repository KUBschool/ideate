Bad behavior toward service workers is depressingly common, but new research finds it has an unexpected side effect: it makes bystanders more generous toward the worker who was mistreated, not just more annoyed at the person being rude.

Across four studies, participants who witnessed a server, cashier or other service worker treated with incivility by another customer went on to tip that worker noticeably more than participants who saw a neutral interaction — tips rose from an average of roughly 17 percent to about 20 percent. The effect held even when the witnesses had no direct interaction with the rude customer themselves.

## It's sympathy, not spite

The research digs into why the bump happens, and the answer isn't a desire to make a statement or punish the rude customer — it's straightforward sympathy for the worker. That distinction matters for how businesses think about service recovery: the moment right after a customer behaves badly may be exactly when other customers are primed to reward good service, not just tolerate a bad scene.

> "But how you treat them signals who you are as a person."

For managers, it's a reminder that a single rude customer doesn't have to be a pure loss for the business or the worker on the receiving end — the rest of the room is often quietly rooting for them.
