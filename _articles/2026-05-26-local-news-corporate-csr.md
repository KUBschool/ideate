The collapse of local newspapers over the past two decades is usually discussed in terms of civic life — fewer school-board stories, less accountability journalism at city hall. New research extends that concern directly into the corporate world, finding a measurable link between local news decline and how seriously nearby companies treat corporate social responsibility.

The study connects local newsroom staffing and closures to changes in CSR engagement among companies headquartered in those markets. As local reporting capacity shrinks, the companies that once operated under that scrutiny show a corresponding pullback in CSR commitments — not necessarily through any single decision, but as sustained public attention on their conduct fades.

## Local press as an accountability mechanism, not just coverage

The research frames local journalism as a form of external monitoring that markets and regulators have quietly relied on, alongside formal channels like audits and disclosure requirements. As that monitoring layer erodes in more and more communities, the finding suggests companies may need less formal, more voluntary reasons to maintain the same level of public commitment — a dynamic with implications well beyond any single firm's public image.
