Entrepreneurs are often advised to pivot when market conditions, customer needs or business assumptions prove incorrect. While pivots are widely viewed as critical to startup success, they can also create uncertainty by changing a venture's goals, products, or target markets. Many theories of strategic change suggest that such disruptions may weaken team cohesion and prompt employees or co-founders to leave. This study examines whether those assumptions hold true within new venture teams.

The researchers analyzed team turnover before and after business model pivots to determine whether strategic redirection leads to member departures. Contrary to expectations, they found little evidence that turnover increases during the periods surrounding a pivot. Team members generally remained with the venture both before and after a significant change in direction, indicating that pivots do not typically trigger immediate instability within startup teams.

Importantly, the pattern persisted regardless of the venture's eventual outcome. Teams remained relatively stable even in ventures that were later acquired or ultimately failed. This finding suggests that the decision to stay or leave is not primarily driven by what the future outcome of the venture may become. Instead, team members appear willing to continue supporting the organization through periods of uncertainty and experimentation.

The study challenges a common assumption in entrepreneurship research: that strategic change naturally disrupts internal team dynamics. Rather than viewing pivots as moments of organizational breakdown, the findings point to the importance of relational ties, trust, and shared commitment within entrepreneurial teams. These social connections may help individuals remain engaged even when a venture's direction changes substantially.For founders, investors, and entrepreneurship educators, the results offer a more optimistic view of organizational resilience. While pivots can alter a startup's strategy, product, or market approach, they do not necessarily undermine team stability. Strong relationships within new venture teams may provide an important foundation for adapting to change, enabling ventures to pursue new opportunities without experiencing immediate losses in talent or commitment.
