Most undergraduate finance courses teach portfolio theory in the abstract. FIN 411, KU's honors investment class, does it with real money on the line — partnering directly with KU Endowment to work through a live allocation question rather than a textbook case study.

This semester's question was pointed: with equity markets trading at rich valuations, does it still make sense for the endowment to lean as heavily into stocks as it has? Students built out the analysis the way an institutional investment committee would — examining historical valuation cycles, stress-testing downside scenarios, and weighing the endowment's long time horizon against near-term risk.

## Why this kind of course matters

The partnership gives students something a simulated portfolio can't: accountability to a real institution making a real decision. For KU Endowment, it's a low-cost way to pressure-test its own assumptions against a fresh set of eyes each year — a small but genuine example of the cross-disciplinary, hands-on approach that runs through KU Business's programs at every level.
