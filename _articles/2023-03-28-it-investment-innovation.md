Companies know they need to innovate to stay competitive. New research suggests many are underinvesting in exactly what makes that innovation possible.

"What we find is investments in IT systems and in IT human resources is critical to maximize innovation across multiple categories," said Adi Masli, professor of accounting and Koch Foundation Fellow at the University of Kansas.

His paper, "Complementarity Between Investment in Information Technology (IT) and IT Human Resources: Implications for Different Types of Firm Innovation," co-written with Feng Guo of Iowa State University, Yijun Li of Erasmus University Rotterdam and Likoebe Maruping of Georgia State University, appears in Information Systems Research.

## Two investments that work better together

Using a sample of 36,812 firm-year observations, the team found that firms investing in both tangible IT systems — software, hardware, networks — and the workplace environment for the IT employees who run them produced significantly more patents, with those patents carrying greater market value.

> "There is a complementarity piece to investments in IT."

The effect held across incremental innovation (built on existing knowledge) and radical innovation (built on genuinely new knowledge) alike — a broader result than Masli's team initially expected, since they anticipated the effect would be concentrated in one category or the other.

## Measuring something companies don't have to disclose

Since firms aren't required to disclose anything about the quality of their IT workplace environment, Masli's team built their measure from an unlikely source: Computerworld magazine's annual "Best Places to Work in IT" list, cross-referenced against factors like work/life balance, promotion opportunity and diversity. Innovation output, in turn, was tracked through patent counts and the market-value response to each new patent — a way of capturing not just how much a company innovates, but how much the market thinks that innovation is worth.
