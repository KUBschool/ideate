Most conversations about delivery efficiency focus on routing — the shortest path, the best sequence of stops. Less attention goes to what happens at each stop itself: whether a driver can actually find a place to park. New research argues that gap deserves more attention as delivery volume keeps climbing.

The study examines how curb space, parking time and delivery-stop duration function as a shared, limited resource in dense urban areas — one that delivery companies, rideshare drivers, and everyday parkers are all competing for simultaneously. That competition intensifies sharply during major events, when a surge of visitors, street closures and temporary demand can overwhelm a city's ordinary curb capacity within a matter of hours.

## A planning problem cities are still catching up to

For cities, the research points toward curb management — not just traffic management — as an increasingly important piece of infrastructure planning, particularly around large scheduled events where the strain is predictable in advance. For delivery companies, it's a reminder that the last hundred feet of a delivery route can be the most expensive part of the whole trip.
