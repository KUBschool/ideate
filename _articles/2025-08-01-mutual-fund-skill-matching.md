The mutual fund industry spends heavily on hiring analysts and portfolio managers with strong general credentials, but new research suggests firms may be underrating a subtler factor: fit. Matching the specific skills an employee brings to the specific demands of their role turns out to matter as much as raw talent for how well a fund performs.

Drawing on detailed employment and performance data across the industry, the research finds that funds with better skill-to-role alignment among their staff post stronger risk-adjusted returns than funds with similarly credentialed but more mismatched teams. The effect holds even after accounting for fund size, strategy, and manager tenure.

## A staffing lesson beyond finance

The finding lines up with a broader body of organizational research suggesting that "who's the most talented" is often the wrong question compared to "who's the best fit for this specific job." For an industry built on identifying mispriced assets, it's a reminder that the same kind of mismatch can exist quietly inside a firm's own org chart.
