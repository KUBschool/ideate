"The Social Network." "WeCrashed." "Silicon Valley." Startup dramas love a co-founder feud. New research asks whether the conventional wisdom behind all that drama — that founders need partners — actually holds up.

"They start to have different ideas, different directions, different moral codes," said Todd Hall, assistant professor of business at the University of Kansas, of the co-founder conflict that drives so much startup fiction. "Generally, people think it's better to found as a team, but there are situations when founding alone can be just as good."

His article, "Lone genius or lonely fool? Exploring the viability of solo-founding in entrepreneurship," investigates that dynamic directly. While conventional wisdom favors co-founder teams, Hall's research finds that partnership can introduce its own destructive conflict — creating as many problems as it solves.

## Three real disadvantages of going alone

Hall doesn't dismiss the case for co-founders. Solo founders face genuine structural disadvantages: weaker access to networks ("A lot of entrepreneurship is, 'I know this person. I know that person.' As a solo founder, you might not have those wide-ranging options," he said), a signaling problem with investors and customers who may read solo status itself as a red flag, and a narrower knowledge base than a well-chosen co-founder could provide.

> "If you bring in a co-founder who has more experience in this field that you don't have, that can really help you make better decisions to grow your company."

## The experience profile that changes the calculus

What Hall's research adds is a condition under which those disadvantages shrink substantially: solo founders with "T-shaped" experience — knowledge that runs deep in one specific area while also spanning a broad set of adjacent skills — perform meaningfully closer to co-founder teams than solo founders without that profile. The practical implication isn't that co-founders are unnecessary, but that the blanket rule enforced by many accelerators and VCs (no solo founders, full stop) may be filtering out exactly the founders equipped to succeed without a partner.
